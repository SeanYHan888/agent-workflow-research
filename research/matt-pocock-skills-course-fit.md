## Two important composition checks

**Review coverage must match the deliverable.** The inspected `implement` orders review before its final commit, while `code-review` specifies `git diff <fixed-point>...HEAD`. That committed comparison does not include newly unstaged, staged-but-uncommitted or untracked work. Depending on prior commits, it can be empty or review an older subset. This is an inference from composing the two instructions, not a reproduced runtime failure. In the learner lab, deliberately choose either a working-tree review that explicitly includes every intended file, or a scoped candidate commit followed by review of that commit. Record the candidate revision and recheck later changes. Sources: [implementation sequence](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/c55ee46073ed923f86ce59a5eb3b6d895095d1b7/skills/engineering/implement/SKILL.md), [review comparison](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/c55ee46073ed923f86ce59a5eb3b6d895095d1b7/skills/engineering/code-review/SKILL.md).

**Read the body, not just the overview.** The repository overview describes TDD using red–green–refactor language. The inspected TDD body explicitly moves refactoring to review. The local installation matches that body; this discrepancy is between overview and detailed procedure, not evidence of an outdated local installation. Sources: [overview](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills), [TDD rules](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/c55ee46073ed923f86ce59a5eb3b6d895095d1b7/skills/engineering/tdd/SKILL.md).

## Proposed teaching flow

Use an existing M2/M3 runner feature, such as a bounded timeout, when the student's readiness and owning Obsidian note support it. This is an example sequence, not a new assignment or schedule.

1. **Inspect and preserve:** student identifies branch, staged/unstaged/untracked work and recovery checkpoint using M11 E1/E2.
2. **Explain the requested behavior:** student predicts success, failure and cancellation cases. Use selected grilling/domain-modeling questions; write an ADR only if a meaningful design tradeoff warrants one.
3. **Specify and slice:** student critiques the specification and chooses an observable test interface. One small feature may need no ticket hierarchy. A larger change can use verifiable slices with explicit prerequisites.
4. **Attempt and test:** student writes or directs one test, predicts its failure, and implements a small change. The teacher gives feedback without taking over the exercise.
5. **Review the actual candidate:** inspect the intended files and revision; compare both behavior and standards. Seed a defect so the student must explain a concrete trigger, consequence and correction.
6. **Accept and recover:** student assembles M11's acceptance packet, makes an accept/revise decision, and demonstrates a controlled recovery. A subsequent unfamiliar modification checks learner understanding.

This uses the existing M11 allocation within **15 hours/week**. No additional required milestone or finish date is implied. A focused reading can replace equivalent material; a full implementation project requires the normal course allocation. Authority, progress ownership and assessment remain governed by the [charter](../course/CHARTER.md), [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md) and [M11 module](../course/modules/engineering-practice.md).

## What the course must still teach explicitly

The selected skills focus on authoring, design, feedback and review; this survey does not establish a complete Git/GitHub or software-delivery curriculum. Keep the complementary [engineering reading map](../materials/engineering-practice.md): index/working-tree/history reasoning, recovery of saved commits, shared-history correction, conflict resolution, PR and CI interpretation, review freshness, acceptance versus merge/deployment, and external-state recovery. These are learning requirements, not gaps to fill by installing more skills automatically.

Our final acceptance standard stays broader than “the skill finished”: request and candidate identity, relevant observed checks, review findings resolved, explicit limits, recovery method, and the student's own explanation plus unfamiliar-change performance. Provider setup, issue publication, commits, merges and deployment remain separate actions with their actual repository/task authorization.
