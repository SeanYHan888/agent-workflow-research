# Teaching office

The [charter](../course/CHARTER.md) owns the teaching agreement; the [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md) owns progression. This directory holds session preparation, original handouts and the [decision history](DECISIONS.md). Detailed teaching follows the active module's evidence.

## Prepare and teach

1. Read the owning Obsidian project's latest attempt, artifact and state; check entry evidence and available hours. Choose one ready increment from the [project route](../ROADMAP.md#project-route).
2. Present a short build brief: useful behavior, one observable acceptance check, learner action, timebox and owner. Identify the milestone criterion and the concrete question the work will answer.
3. Ask the learner to predict and attempt the change or investigation. Inspect their output; use questions and the smallest useful hint before adding an explanation or worked example.
4. Teach the missing mechanism at the point of need. Select one source passage, verifying its revision/section when assigned, then return to the same artifact for a new attempt. Retain explicit resource-completion work inside the budget.
5. Have the learner demonstrate the behavior, inspect a relevant failure and explain what happened. For assessment, introduce a fresh variation under the [rubric](../course/ASSESSMENT.md). A session can end with a well-understood failure and a next hypothesis.
6. Record actual time, artifact/output, assistance and the smallest remaining gap in the owning project. Reuse valid evidence; select one next step. Route new interests through [idea intake](../course/IDEAS.md), naming displaced work.

For a typical 90-minute session, use 10 minutes to inspect evidence and define the check, 15 for a learner prediction/first attempt, 15 for focused explanation or reading, 40 for implementation/debugging and a failure experiment, and 10 for demonstration and reflection. Adapt to the learner; these are teaching defaults, not extra deadlines. Ask one question at a time and wait for the attempt instead of generating the solution or a full lesson sequence.

At weekly review, inspect a demonstrable increment or reproducible experiment and its explanation. Reading supports that result. Check the charter's allocation and explicit completion goals; if the slice cannot fit, narrow it or split future execution work before activation.
